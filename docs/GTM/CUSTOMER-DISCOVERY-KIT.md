# Soma — Customer Discovery Kit

*Copy-paste ready. Built for pre-revenue, research-mode founder outreach. Every
template here is written to the Soma honesty ethos: **only video → brain-activation
is validated** (Meta's public TRIBE v2 encoder, benchmarked vs real fMRI —
not ours, we say so). **activation → attention / engagement is a tested hypothesis,
not a claim.** Nothing in this kit asks you to invent traction, quote a customer
you don't have, or dress a hypothesis as a result. If a line ever tempts you to,
delete it — an honest "we're pre-launch, learning how you test creative" outperforms
a fabricated chart with the skeptical, technical buyers we want.*

**What Soma is (the one-liner you can say out loud and defend):** Upload a video
ad; Soma predicts an average viewer's per-second brain-activation arc from the file
(via Meta's open TRIBE v2 encoder) and flags predicted weak spots — "test your ad
before you spend." No panel, no webcam, minutes not days.

**Golden rule for all of this:** you are doing *research*, not *selling*. The goal
of every touch below is a 20-minute conversation about **how they test creative
today** — their past behavior and real problems — not a demo, not a close.

## 2. Outreach templates (honest, research-mode — NOT a pitch)

Two cold templates. Both: research framing, no fake traction, ask for 20 minutes on
**how they test creative today**. Fill the `[brackets]`. Keep it short — long cold
messages read as sales.

### A. Cold email

> **Subject:** how do you decide which creative to run?
>
> Hi [First name],
>
> I'm [Name], one of the founders of Soma (very early — pre-launch). We're
> building a tool that predicts a viewer's second-by-second reaction to a video ad
> from the file, to flag likely weak spots before you spend on it. Meta open-sourced
> the brain-response model underneath; we're building the read-out on top and, honestly,
> still validating how far it goes.
>
> Before we build more, I'm trying to learn how teams like yours actually decide
> which creative to run and what happens when an ad underperforms. Not selling
> anything — I don't even have a demo to push on you — just want to understand the
> real workflow from someone who lives it.
>
> Would you be open to 20 minutes in the next week or two? I'll come with questions,
> not a pitch. Totally fine if not.
>
> Thanks either way,
> [Name]
> [optional: one line of who you are — "CS at Columbia, previously founding eng at Krypton AI"]

*Notes:* the subject is a real question, not a hook. "pre-launch," "still
validating," "don't even have a demo to push" are all true and disarming. Never add
a fake stat, logo, or "join 100+ brands."

### B. LinkedIn DM (shorter — connect note or first message)

> Hi [First name] — I'm a founder researching how performance/creative teams decide
> which video ad to actually run (and what happens when one flops). Genuinely
> research mode, not a pitch — we're pre-launch. You're exactly the kind of person I
> should learn from. Open to a 20-min call in the next couple weeks? No worries if
> not.

*If they reply with interest, follow up:*

> Amazing, thank you. I mostly want to hear how you work today — how you pick a cut,
> how you test, what "underperforming" looks like for you. Here's my Calendly:
> [link]. Or just drop a couple times that suit you.

*Notes:* under ~60 words for the first touch (LinkedIn truncates and long DMs feel
like spam). No product claim at all. If they ask "what does it do?" answer the
honest one-liner, then steer back: *"Happy to show you later — right now I'd rather
learn how you do it, so we build the right thing."*

## 3. Discovery call script — 20 minutes, Mom-Test style

**The Mom Test rule:** ask about their **past behavior and real problems**, never
about your idea or the future. Good questions are ones they could answer even if
your product never existed. If you catch yourself describing Soma, stop — you've
left discovery and entered pitching.

### Explicit DON'Ts (read before every call)
- **Don't pitch.** They didn't take this call to hear about you. If they ask, give the one honest line and return to their world.
- **Don't ask leading questions.** ❌ "Wouldn't it be useful to catch weak ads before spending?" → everyone says yes, it's worthless. ✅ "Tell me about the last ad that underperformed — what happened?"
- **Don't ask hypotheticals.** ❌ "Would you use a tool that…" / "How much would you pay for…" → fantasy answers. Ask what they did last time.
- **Don't ask "would you buy this?"** Opinions about the future are polite lies. Look for what they *already do, pay for, and hack around*.
- **Don't accept compliments as data.** "That's cool / clever / I'd love to try it" is politeness, not a signal (see §5). Dig for the underlying behavior instead.
- **Don't fill silence.** Ask, then shut up. The best answers come after the pause.
- **Don't correct their process or teach.** You're here to learn, not to be right.

### Timing map (20 min)
- 0–2 min — frame & permission
- 2–7 min — how they decide which creative to run (behavior)
- 7–11 min — testing spend & pre-flight QA today (money + process)
- 11–15 min — what happens when an ad underperforms (the pain, concrete)
- 15–18 min — prior tools: attention / neuro / testing (what they tried, why they stopped)
- 18–20 min — what it would take to trust a tool + referrals & permission to follow up

### 0–2 min — Frame
> "Thanks for the time. Quick context: we're very early and I'm not here to pitch —
> I genuinely want to understand how you decide which creative to run and how you
> test it. There are no right answers; the more concrete and real, the better. Mind
> if I take notes? Everything stays between us."

### 2–7 min — How they decide which creative to run (behavior, not opinion)
- "Walk me through the last video ad you launched. How did you decide *that* was the one to run?"
- "When you've got several cuts or variants of the same ad, how do you pick which goes live?"
- "What signals do you trust before spend — hook rate, gut, past winners, someone's opinion?"
- "Who else weighs in, and who has final say?"
- "How often does the one you picked turn out to be the wrong call?"

### 7–11 min — Testing spend & pre-flight QA today (get to money & process)
- "How do you test creative right now — before or with real spend? Walk me through it."
- "Roughly how much time and budget goes into testing / vetting an ad before it scales?"
- "Do you burn spend to learn which variant wins, or check anything before it goes live?"
- "Any tools, spreadsheets, panels, or people you pay for in that step? What do they cost?"
- "What's annoying or slow about how you do it today?"

*(Money and existing tools are the strongest signals — a budget that already exists
is worth more than any enthusiasm about the future.)*

### 11–15 min — What happens when an ad underperforms (the concrete pain)
- "Tell me about the last ad that underperformed. What happened, start to finish?"
- "How did you find out it was underperforming, and how fast?"
- "What did that cost you — spend, time, a client conversation, a relaunch?"
- "Could anyone have seen it coming before you spent? What would have had to be different?"
- "How often does that happen — every week, every campaign?"

### 15–18 min — Prior tools: attention / neuro / testing
- "Have you ever tried an attention-prediction, eye-tracking, neuro, or ad-scoring tool — Realeyes, Neurons, System1, Motion, anything? What made you try it?"
- "What happened — did it stick? Why or why not?"
- "What did it get right, and where did you stop trusting it?"
- "If a tool gave you a per-second read on an ad, what would make it credible vs. noise you'd ignore?"

*(This surfaces the real bar for trust and the graveyard of tools they abandoned —
gold for you.)*

### 18–20 min — Trust bar, referrals, permission
- "Last one: for you to actually put a new pre-flight tool into your workflow, what would it have to prove first?"
- "Who are two people you respect who think hard about creative testing? A quick intro would mean a lot."
- "Can I come back to you as we learn more? And would you ever be up for looking at an early version on one of your own ads?" *(bridge to the LOI — only if the conversation earned it; never force it.)*

**If they ask what Soma does:** *"Honest version: you upload a video ad, and we
predict the average viewer's second-by-second brain-activation arc from the file
using Meta's open model, then flag likely weak spots. The video→brain-activation
step is validated and benchmarked; whether that predicts attention and engagement
is exactly what we're testing right now — I won't claim it does until a held-out
test says so."* Then return to their workflow.

## 4. Design-partner LOI template

Short, non-binding, mutual. Free early access + one co-tested ad, in exchange for
feedback and permission to reference. Logo use is **optional and separate** — never
a condition. This is a handshake to work together, not a contract; keep a lawyer's
version for anything binding later.

> **Soma — Design Partner Letter of Intent (non-binding)**
>
> **Between:** [Your Company / Brand] ("Partner") and Soma ("Soma").
> **Date:** [date]
>
> This is a friendly, **non-binding** letter of intent to explore working together.
> It creates no payment obligation and no exclusivity for either side, and either
> party can walk away at any time.
>
> **What Soma provides:**
> - Free early access to Soma during the design-partner period.
> - We'll run Soma on **one of the Partner's real video ads** together and walk
>   through the output with you.
> - Direct line to the founders; your feedback shapes the roadmap.
>
> **What the Partner provides:**
> - Candid feedback: what's useful, what's missing, what you'd never trust.
> - ~2–3 short feedback sessions over [4–6] weeks.
> - [Optional] Permission for Soma to **reference [Company] as a design partner**
>   (name and/or logo) in investor and product materials. This is optional and can
>   be revoked in writing anytime — the partnership does not depend on it.
>
> **Honesty commitment (Soma's promise to you):** Soma will always show you which
> parts of its output are **validated** (today: video → brain-activation, via Meta's
> open TRIBE v2 model, benchmarked against real fMRI) versus a **labeled hypothesis
> we're still testing** (that this predicts attention / engagement). We will not
> present a hypothesis as a proven result to you or about you.
>
> **Confidentiality:** Each side keeps the other's non-public information
> confidential. Soma will not share the Partner's ad creative or performance data
> externally without written permission.
>
> **Not binding:** This LOI is an expression of mutual intent only. It is not a
> contract, purchase order, or commitment to buy or sell.
>
> Partner: __________________  Name/Title: __________  Date: ______
> Soma: _____________________  Name/Title: __________  Date: ______

*Usage notes:* offer this **only** after a call where the pain and interest were
real (see §5). Lead with the free value ("let's run it on one of your actual ads"),
treat the logo as a nice-to-have, and keep the whole thing to one page. If they
hesitate on referencing them, drop that clause instantly — a private design partner
who actually engages is worth more than a logo you can show.

### What counts as a real signal vs. politeness
**Real signals (they cost the person something — money, time, or specificity):**
- They walk you through a **specific recent event** in detail ("last Tuesday we scaled a cut that tanked and I lost $4k before I killed it") — concreteness = truth.
- They **already spend money or time** on the problem: a tool, a panel, an agency line item, or hours of manual pre-flight QA. An existing budget is the single strongest signal.
- They've **built a workaround** — a testing spreadsheet, a manual hook-rate ritual, a "burn $X to find the winner" habit. People only hack around real pain.
- They **make a real commitment in the room**: a calendar date, an intro they actually send, sharing one of their ads, agreeing to the LOI. Commitment of time or reputation > words.
- They give a **substantive objection** ("I wouldn't trust it unless it beat my hook-rate benchmark on my own past winners") — engaged skepticism means they're modeling real use.

**Politeness / false positives (discount to ~zero):**
- "That's so cool / clever / I love this." Compliments feel great and mean nothing. Say thanks, then ask, *"When did you last run into that problem?"*
- "I'd definitely use that" / "we'd totally buy it" — a hypothetical about the future. Only past behavior and money on the table count.
- Generic enthusiasm with **no specific example** behind it.
- "Send me info / let's stay in touch" with **no date** — a soft no. Try once for a concrete next step; if none, log it as a no and move on.
- Advice about your business model or fundraising instead of talking about **their** problem — pleasant, but it's them avoiding the real answer.

**Decision rule:** an LOI or a follow-up is earned by the **Signal Ledger**, not by
how nice the call felt. If a call has zero ledger checks but three compliments,
it's a **no** — record it honestly and keep hunting. Faking warmth in your notes
only wastes your own runway.
