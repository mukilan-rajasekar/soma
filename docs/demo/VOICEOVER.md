# /demo — voiceover script (~60s)

Timed for a single top-to-bottom scroll of `usesoma.work/demo`. Each beat is pinned to a
section; the target time is when you should *finish* that beat. Every section animates on
entry, so land on it, let the animation play, then move.

Delivery note: don't read the on-screen copy back. The voiceover is the layer *over* it.

---

## The thing to understand before you record

**The page has twelve sections. The take narrates six of them.** Those are two different
lists, and conflating them is how a 60-second take runs to ninety.

Nothing is deleted from the page. A partner who opens the link cold and never watches the
video reads all twelve — the methodology in §05/§06, the service ladder, the buyer's
checklist, the moat counters — and that reader is who they are for. The take has a
different job: make one person believe the read, then show them the two things the read is
*for*.

The page is a three-act story now, and the take follows it:

| Act | The claim | Sections |
|---|---|---|
| Measure | We can tell a winning cut from a losing one | hero, §01–§04 |
| Generate | So we can build the winner from a sentence | §07 |
| Edit | And change it in a sentence, re-scored | §08 |

Acts two and three are new, and they are the point. Everything in act one exists to make
them credible; none of it is the payoff. Budget accordingly: **act one gets 26 seconds,
acts two and three get 25 between them.**

## The script

**[0:00 — Hero · "Anyone can make a hundred ads"]**  (~8s)
> "Making ads is solved. Knowing which one wins isn't. Soma reads how a brain watches the
> cut — and then builds on that read."

*Land, let the hero arc draw. Don't narrate the three-act strip underneath — it reads
itself, and it is doing the job of telling a cold viewer what the next 50 seconds contain.*

**[0:08 — §01 The science]**  (~5s)
> "Attention isn't one thing. One network steers focus, another fires on surprise. We
> measure them separately."

*Cut from the old take: the region names, and the eye-tracking comparison. The two-region
brain does that work on screen without you.*

**[0:13 — §02 Hook scoring]**  (~5s)
> "The first three seconds are the hook. Here, surprise spikes two seconds in — the opening
> lands."

*Dropped: "worth almost half the total." Keep the spike callout; it is the one moment a
viewer sees a claim and its evidence in the same frame.*

**[0:18 — §03 Comprehension]**  (~4s)
> "Then: did the product actually register? Soma reads the ad's own words, on screen and
> out loud."

**[0:22 — §04 Ranked batch]**  (~4s)
> "Ten ads for one product, ranked — and every score broken into why."

*Dropped: the ingredient-breakdown paradox. It is the best story on the page and it needs
eight seconds. Save it for the live conversation.*

**[0:26 — bridge · through §05 and §06, no pause]**  (~3s)
> "Then shot by shot: the whole ad re-scored without each one."

*A moving shot, not a beat. You are travelling past the five overlaid arcs and the
eleven-thumbnail filmstrip at speed. Both are load-bearing setup for what comes next — the
five cuts become the five candidates in §07, the per-shot deltas become the candidate edits
in §08 — so they must be seen and must not be stopped on.*

**[0:29 — §07 Generation]**  (~12s)
> "Now the other direction. Write a brief — and Soma generates against your brand kit, and
> against what it knows makes hooks land."
>
> *(beat — let the columns rise and the pass bar drop)*
>
> "Every candidate is scored before you ever see it. Nineteen of twenty-four never reach
> you. Generating is cheap. The filter is the product."

*The section runs a 5.2-second clock: the brief types itself, twenty-four columns rise, the
pass bar drops, nineteen dim below it, five land as ranked cards. Stop with the chart and
the board both in frame — the section is built so they fit one screen together.*

**[0:41 — §08 AI ad editing]**  (~13s)
> "Editing is the same box, in plain language. Ask for the change —"
>
> *(beat — let the three options land)*
>
> "— and Soma finds three ways to do it, scores each one, applies the best. Sixty-two to
> sixty-six. The shot drops out of the timeline, the arc redraws shorter, and nothing was
> re-rendered. The frames you shot stay the frames you shot."

*The strongest single beat in the take. Do not rush the filmstrip collapse.*

**[0:54 — §09 Watch it live]**  (~3s)
> "And you can watch any score track the frames it's reading."

*Press play. Three seconds of playhead travel is enough. This is the take's only click.*

**[0:57 — CTA]**  (~3s)
> "Measure, generate, edit. That's Soma."

*Mirrors the three-act strip under the hero. Land on it and stop.*

## What got cut from the take, and what it costs

Every row below stays on the page. It stops being narrated.

| Section | Why it's cut | What it costs |
|---|---|---|
| §05 Compare the cuts | §07's board is the same five cuts, and says so on the page | The apples-to-apples framing. Recovered visually in the bridge. |
| §06 Shot diagnosis | §08 renders the same filmstrip and the same deltas | The leave-one-shot-out method. §08 shows the result of it instead. |
| The read-out recap | Restates §02/§03 on the hero ad | Nothing. It is a recap by construction. |
| §10 The moat | The counters need a pause to be read | The corpus and the flywheel — the most painful cut for an investor. Say it in the room, not on the tape. |
| §11 Service ladder | Needs ~8s to be worth anything | How we make money, and how concierge works. Expensive for a YC audience: have it ready as the first thing you say when the video ends. |
| §12 Buyer's checklist | Needs reading, not watching | The competitive framing. |

**If you get 90 seconds instead of 60:** buy §10 and §11 back, in that order. The moat and
the service ladder are what a partner asks about anyway.

## The tighter 45s cut

Drop §03 comprehension and §09 watch-it-live. Compress act one to hero → §01 → §02 → §04
in fifteen seconds total, then run §07 and §08 at full length. Act one is compressible; the
two new acts are not, and cutting into them defeats the purpose of the take.

## The lines that matter most

If a partner only remembers one sentence:
> "Making ads is solved. Knowing which one wins isn't — Soma reads how a brain watches each
> cut, then builds and edits against that read."

If they remember two, the second is:
> "Generating is cheap. The filter is the product."
